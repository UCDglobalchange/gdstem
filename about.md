# About GDSTEM

The **Global Dynamical and Structural Terrestrial Ecosystem Model (GDSTEM)** is a process-based terrestrial ecosystem model developed to investigate interactions among terrestrial ecosystem structure, biogeochemistry, environmental change, and human land use.

GDSTEM is under active development by a collaborative team of ecosystem scientists and model developers. The project builds on more than three decades of development and application of the **Terrestrial Ecosystem Model (TEM)** and its successors.

---

## Scientific lineage

GDSTEM is part of a model lineage that began with the **Terrestrial Ecosystem Model (TEM)** in the early 1990s.

TEM was developed by researchers at the Ecosystems Center of the Marine Biological Laboratory, including Jerry Melillo, David Kicklighter, Dave McGuire, and collaborators. An important feature of TEM was its process-based representation of coupled terrestrial carbon and nitrogen dynamics, allowing researchers to investigate ecosystem responses to changes in climate, atmospheric CO₂, and other environmental drivers at regional to global scales.

Over subsequent decades, TEM was extended and applied to an increasingly broad range of questions involving terrestrial biogeochemistry, climate change, atmospheric chemistry, hydrology, and land-use change.

---

## From TEM to TEM-Hydro

**TEM-Hydro** expanded the TEM framework with a more detailed treatment of ecosystem hydrology and carbon-water interactions. These developments provided part of the scientific and computational foundation for the subsequent development of GDSTEM.

---

## From TEM-Hydro to GDSTEM

GDSTEM builds on this foundation while substantially expanding the representation of terrestrial ecosystem structure and land-use dynamics.

A central development is the use of a **cohort-based representation of vegetation**. Rather than representing each location as a single homogeneous ecosystem, GDSTEM can track vegetation cohorts with different ages and land-use histories. This provides a framework for representing the long-term ecological consequences of disturbance and land-use change.

GDSTEM also incorporates contemporary global environmental datasets and simulation workflows designed for regional and global applications.

The resulting model provides a framework for studying how climate, atmospheric composition, land use, ecosystem history, and other environmental drivers interact to shape terrestrial carbon, nitrogen, and water dynamics.

---

## GDSTEM today

GDSTEM is currently undergoing continued development, calibration, and evaluation. Current efforts include improvements to model processes, systematic benchmarking and calibration, and expanded representation of ecosystem disturbance.

The model is being applied to global terrestrial carbon-cycle research and has contributed simulations to international carbon-cycle assessments, including the **Global Carbon Budget**.

A comprehensive description and evaluation of the current model is in preparation. Additional scientific results and model documentation will be added to this website as that work progresses.

## Development and collaboration

GDSTEM is developed as a collaborative scientific modeling project. Researchers interested in the model, its development, or potential scientific collaborations are encouraged to contact members of the development team.
